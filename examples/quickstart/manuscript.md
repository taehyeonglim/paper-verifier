## Introduction

Conversational agents can scaffold learner attention in ways static materials
cannot (Chen & Park, 2023). Rivera et al. (2022) showed that immediate
feedback timing improves retention in undergraduate samples. Engagement is
commonly measured with short self-report scales (Novak, 2024).

## Methods

32 undergraduates were recruited (N = 32) for course credit.
Participants were randomly assigned to study a short statistics lesson either
with an AI tutor chatbot (AI) or as plain static reading material (TEXT).
Two participants were excluded due to incomplete data, yielding a final analytic sample of N = 30.
The engagement scale showed good reliability (Cronbach's α = .81).

## Results

Students in the AI condition reported higher engagement (*M* = 5.42, *SD* = 0.83) than students in the TEXT condition (*M* = 4.91, *SD* = 0.95).
The condition effect on engagement was significant, *F*(1, 28) = 5.21, *p* = .030, with β = 0.51, 95% CI [0.07, 0.95].
A Wilcoxon signed-rank robustness check confirmed the engagement effect, *V* = 312, *p* = .026, *r* = .41, d_z = 0.43.
Quiz accuracy was higher under AI (*M* = 78.4%, *SD* = 10.2) than under TEXT (*M* = 71.2%, *SD* = 11.5), *F*(1, 28) = 4.87, *p* = .036.

## Discussion

The pattern is consistent with prior findings on conversational scaffolding
(Chen & Park, 2023): guided dialogue appears to hold attention on the lesson
content, which in turn supports quiz performance.
